Executive summary
The 60/10/2 three-stage platform is ten years into production and the facility, not the reservoir, sets
the rate. The wells can deliver about 163.4 MMscfd of gas equivalent; the facility passes 137.1 MMscfd.
The first constraint is the HP separator at 99.8 % of its Souders-Brown gas load, the second the export
compressor at 98.1 % of its 2.3 MW driver, and the third — the one a gas-side package would miss —
the produced-water plant at 92.7 % of 3,000 bbl/d. The flare is at 91.1 % of its mass capacity and is the
relief gate, not a production gate, until the gas rate rises.

Seven fixes were screened and six packages valued over ten years. The recommended package is the
HIPPS + water package: an export-compressor re-rate, HP separator internals, a SIL-3 HIPPS loop and
a produced-water upgrade with coalescer. It fits the four-week shutdown, costs 36 MUSD, returns 24.8
MUSD after tax at a 10 % discount, pays back in year one, and raises the plateau from 137.1 to 161.8
MMscfd and condensate from 2,936 to about 3,464 bbl/d (+24.7 MMscfd and +528 bbl/d). The flare and
header upgrade is the fallback at 42 MUSD and 19.2 MUSD NPV.

The package is a gas project with a condensate co-product. Of 98.2 MUSD of discounted revenue, 72.4
MUSD is gas and 25.8 MUSD is condensate; on gas alone the package is about −0.5 MUSD and on
condensate alone −36.2 MUSD. The client's condensate-only objective does not pay at 70 USD/bbl. The
recommendation is conditional on selling the incremental gas at the assumed 4 USD/MMBtu.

1 Introduction and scope
1.1 Purpose
This report is the Front-End Engineering Design (FEED) study for debottlenecking the 60/10/2 offshore
platform. It follows the diagnosis in notebook 08: the field is ten years into production, the wells make
more gas and water and less condensate than the design case, and the client wants more condensate
without exceeding the flare limit or operating outside the design envelope. The study identifies the
capacity chain, sizes the relief and separator checks with the azoth library, screens seven fixes and six
packages, values them with a ten-year incremental discounted cash-flow model, and recommends a
scope that fits the next four-week shutdown.

The report is one of four deliverables: the study itself, a drawing set (block flow diagram, simplified
P&ID and schedule), a calculation workbook (basis, capacity, equipment, cost, economics, plan and
verification), and this narrative. It is written as a case study for the azoth repository and doubles as a
worked example of the library's process, safety and cost calculations.

1.2 Scope and battery limits
   Facility scope: HP, MP and LP separation, export compression and cooling, produced-water treatment,
    condensate pumping, the HP relief path (PSV, flare header and tip) and the export gas and liquid lines.
   Time scope: the year-ten operating point with a ten-year production forecast; a single four-week
    shutdown window.
   Option scope: seven fixes and six packages; the fourth stage and stabiliser are carried as add-ons and
    priced, but not recommended.
   Excluded: reservoir simulation, flow assurance (hydrates, wax, slugging, sand), decommissioning,
    inflation and foreign exchange, detailed design, and any work outside the battery limits in Figure 7-1.

1.3 Basis and limitations
The field, the production history, the costs and the prices are illustrative; no number here should be
read as a real asset. The thermodynamic and equipment numbers are azoth library results,
reproduced from notebook 08 by model/case_model.py. The library reports them as partially_verified
— partly externally checked — so they are screening values and evidence for a finding, not design
deliverables. Section 10.3 lists each calculation and its status.

The cost estimate is AACE Class 4/5 in year-ten money, built from brownfield allowances rather than a
greenfield module stack. It carries no contingency, escalation, owner's costs or detailed design, and its
accuracy is correspondingly wide. Section 6 sets out the basis and exclusions.

1.4 Report structure
Section 2 fixes the basis of design. Section 3 diagnoses the facility: the capacity chain, the relief case
and the safety case. Section 4 screens the options and selects the package. Section 5 specifies the
equipment. Section 6 estimates the cost. Section 7 presents the drawings. Section 8 is the
implementation plan. Section 9 values the project. Section 10 collects the risks, assumptions and
verification status, and Section 11 concludes. The appendices hold the calculation register, datasheets,
cost detail, task list and references.

2 Basis of design
2.1 The facility
The platform is the 60/10/2 three-stage manned facility from the field-development study: 137 MMscfd
of sales gas at design, about 6,100 bbl/d of stock-tank liquid, and no produced water. The actual
history diverged from the design forecast. Pressure support held better than planned but the gas-oil
ratio rose faster, so gas sits at the facility limit while condensate falls and water breaks through. The
HP separator is the first stage of a three-phase retrofit: water is drawn off at the HP vessel, and the MP
and LP stages are two-phase.

At year ten the fluid is gassier. The C7+ fraction is lower and the condensate yield has fallen from 44 to
about 21 bbl/MMscf. Produced water is 2,782 bbl/d against 2,936 bbl/d of condensate, a 48.7 % water
cut. The feed to the HP separator is 2,230 mol/s, of which 13.45 mol % is water.

3 Facility diagnosis
3.1 Process model and let-down
The HP oil is two-phase at 60 bara, so the throttling valve refuses it; the MP stage uses an isenthalpic
flash at 10 bara followed by a separator. The MP liquid is single-phase at 10 bara, so the LP stage can
use the valve. Each stage gas is cooled to 313.15 K and compressed to 90 bara in equal-ratio stages no
larger than 2.5. The stock tank flashes the LP liquid at 288.15 K and 1 atm; its flash gas joins the sales
gas, and the liquid is the condensate product. The model reproduces the design gas rate at the year-
ten point and gives the product slate used throughout this study.

The HP separator's interface settling is 17.1 s against the class's one-minute check, so the produced-
water package must include a coalescer. That is a performance requirement, not a capacity one, and it
does not change the gas cap.

3.2 The capacity chain
The first limit is not the compressor. At the year-ten point the HP separator is at 99.8 % of its Souders-
Brown gas load, the export compression at 98.1 % of its driver, the produced-water plant at 92.7 %
and the flare at 91.1 %. The gas trunk is at 76.2 % and the PSV at 73.5 %; the pump and the liquid line
have ample headroom. The separator is the first constraint, the compressor the second, and the water
plant the hidden third: a gas-side package that ignores the water plant cannot use the capacity it buys.

3.3 The relief case
The governing relief case is the HP separator blocked outlet: the full HP vapour inflow, flashed at the
relieving pressure, 42.63 kg/s. The fire case is 0.54 kg/s, so the blocked outlet governs by nearly two
orders of magnitude. The installed P orifice has 6.38 in² against a required 4.69 in², 73.5 % of its
capacity; at +18 % gas it needs 5.53 in² and is still a P orifice at 86.7 %. The PSV is not the gate.

3.4 The safety case for HIPPS
The HIPPS is a real alternative to the flare upgrade. The SIL-3 function is verifiable against NOG-070
and NORSOK S-001, and the two-barrier pipeline check is satisfied. NOG-070 gives a minimum SIL of 3
for a HIPPS pipeline SIF; a PFD of 1e-4 with 1oo2 voting achieves SIL 3 and complies. The pipeline's
maximum incidental pressure is 72.6 bara against a 66 barg design, which requires two barriers; the
study finds the protection adequate. NORSOK S-001 secondary pressure protection is acceptable with
a 12-month proof test, relief leakage assessed and routed to a safe location.

4.3 Selection and fallback
The recommended package is the HIPPS + water package: the compressor re-rate, the separator
internals, the HIPPS SIL-3 loop and the produced-water upgrade with coalescer. The flare and header
upgrade is the fallback if the SIL-3 safety case is not approved. Both fit the four-week shutdown; the
fallback costs 6 MUSD more and returns 5.6 MUSD less NPV, but it does not depend on the 35 % relief
credit.

The fourth stage is not recommended for this shutdown. Its 5-week duration forces compression into
the window with a 3.3 MUSD labour premium, and its NPV of 15.9 MUSD is below the core package. It
should be revisited at a higher condensate price or in the following shutdown. The stabiliser is
rejected: 8 weeks extends the shutdown by two weeks and defers 11.0 MUSD, leaving 2.6 MUSD NPV.

Neither product alone carries the capital. On gas alone the package is −0.5 MUSD; on condensate
alone it is −36.2 MUSD. That is the study's most important commercial finding: the client's
condensate-only objective does not pay at 70 USD/bbl, and the recommendation is conditional on
selling the incremental gas.

5.2 V-101 — HP separator internals
The HP separator is a 1.96 m × 7.26 m horizontal three-phase vessel at 60 bara and 354.15 K. At the
year-ten point the gas load is 99.8 % of the Souders-Brown capacity at K = 0.11 m/s, with oil retention
of 10.92 min (above the 9 min minimum) and water retention of 7.83 min (above 6.5 min). The
interface settling time is 17.09 s against the class's one-minute check, so the vessel cannot meet the
produced-water duty without a coalescer; that is a performance requirement carried by the water
package, not a capacity fix.

5.3 K-101 — export compressor re-rate
The export compressor lifts HP, MP and LP gas to 90 bara in equal-ratio stages of no more than 2.5,
with interstage cooling to 313.15 K. At the year-ten point it draws 2,256 kW against a 2,300 kW driver
(98.1 %), and at the well potential it would draw 2,689 kW (116.9 %). The re-rate is specified to deliver
+18 % capacity, lifting the compressor cap from 139.7 to 161.8 MMscfd. The three trains are staged 1 +
3 + 5 (HP, MP, LP) at equal ratio. The FEED scope is a vendor study and kit: revised impellers, a
confirmed 2.7 MW driver, a revised compressor curve with anti-surge recycle and an independent
overspeed trip, and the cooler duty increase from 3,885 to 4,630 kW. Torsional, lateral, impeller-stress
and loss-of-utilities cases remain conditions on the vendor (HAZ-09).

The compressor is the long-lead item and the schedule's critical path. The study assumes a 22-week
order-to-delivery for the re-rate kit; the implementation plan places the purchase order at week 8 to
protect the week-32 shutdown. A slip beyond week 8 moves the shutdown, and a two-week slip costs
11.0 MUSD of deferred production.

5.4 HIPPS — SIL-3 high-pressure protection
The HIPPS protects the HP inlet and the 66 barg pipeline from the 95 bara wellhead pressure. The
study specifies two pressure transmitters in 1oo2 voting, a SIL-3 logic solver and a shutdown valve
(SDV-101) on the HP inlet. The function achieves SIL 3 at PFD 1e-4 with a 12-month proof test;
hardware fault tolerance is 1 and diagnostic coverage 0.6. The pipeline maximum incidental pressure
is 72.6 bara, which requires two barriers; NORSOK S-001 secondary pressure protection is acceptable
with relief leakage assessed and routed to a safe location.

The relief credit is 35 % of the blocked-outlet case, taking 50.31 kg/s to 32.70 kg/s, the flare to 69.9 %
and the PSV requirement to 3.60 in². That credit is an assumption. It must be confirmed by a dynamic
relief study and a SIL verification against the actual device failure rates before it is booked; if it is not,
the flare/header upgrade is the fallback and the package still fits the shutdown. Proof testing and any
bypass are restricted to the shutdown, with a rate cap and independent approval; a live 12-month
proof test at full rate is outside the relief basis because the +18 % case reaches 107.5 % of the existing
flare (HAZ-03).

5.5 Produced-water package

capacity to 6,000 bbl/d and adds a coalescer to meet the interface-settling duty. The package
comprises a hydrocyclone (V-105), a coalescer (V-106, 1.2 m ID x 4.0 m T/T, 4.3 m3, 6.5 min retention),
two 50 % treated-water pumps (P-102, 20 m3/h each, 15 kW, one standby), an oil-in-water analyser

5.10 Flash gas, dehydration and let-down protection
Stock-tank flash gas is routed to the fuel-gas system, not the 90 bara sales header; the notebook's
mass balance added it to sales gas, a simplification corrected in the DEXPI PFD. At the year-ten
composition the flash is liquid-only at 15 °C and 1.013 bara, so the fuel-gas stream is small; the routing
still matters because a future gassier stock tank must not back-pressure the sales header.

The compression and cooling train delivers water-saturated gas at 90 bara and 313.15 K. The existing
TEG dehydration unit is outside the debottlenecking battery limits and is assumed to be adequate, but
the higher rate (137.1 to 161.8 MMscfd) and a 3 lb/MMscf pipeline specification have not been
demonstrated. This is an open HAZOP action, not a closed assumption. The same review adds PSV-102
on V-102 and PSV-103 on V-103 for let-down valve fail-open, independent 1oo2 LAHH/LSHH on V-101
to ESD, and fail-closed action on LCV-102. Rev B also adds the ESD hierarchy (ESD-1/2/3), fire and gas
detection at the HP separator, compression and water package, blowdown on V-101 and the HP inlet
spool, and ESDV/SSIV scope on the export lines; the cause-and-effect matrix is a detailed-design
deliverable (HAZ-10).

8 Implementation project plan
8.1 Execution strategy
The plan runs from FEED close-out to benefits realisation in 42 weeks, with the four-week shutdown
starting in week 32. The strategy is to do all pre-shutdown work — detailed design, procurement,

installation, tie-in, loop testing and commissioning. The compressor re-rate is the long-lead item and
the only activity that can move the shutdown.

The recommended package is executed as four parallel work fronts inside the window: the produced-
water package (four weeks, the critical duration), the compressor re-rate (three weeks), the separator
internals (three weeks) and the HIPPS (two weeks), followed by commissioning and handover. The
flare/header fallback, if selected, runs four weeks in parallel with the water package and does not
extend the window.

8.3 Shutdown schedule
The four-week window is enough for the gas side with the water upgrade and for the HIPPS variant:
the longest task is the four-week water package, and the compressor, internals, HIPPS and flare work
sit inside it in parallel. The fourth stage is a five-week job; it is compressed into the window with a 3.3
MUSD labour premium. The stabiliser is eight weeks, so it extends the shutdown by two weeks and
defers production; on the year-ten rates that deferment is 11.0 MUSD before any manhour premium.
The two add-ons together defer 14.3 MUSD.

   Compressor re-rate kit: 22 weeks from purchase order; order by week 8 to deliver before week 32. This

   HIPPS hardware: 12 weeks; order by week 8, factory acceptance test by week 32, loop test inside the

8.5 HSE, commissioning and operations
The shutdown is a simultaneous-operations campaign on a live platform: hydrocarbon isolation, gas-
freeing, hot-work permits and dropped-object controls govern the schedule. The HIPPS trip must be
proof-tested before the HP inlet is returned to service, and the PSV must be re-certified.
Commissioning includes leak tests, loop checks, compressor surge and performance tests, separator
and water-package performance tests, and a flare radiation check before the first relief event.

Operational readiness requires revised operating procedures, alarm and trip setpoints, training for
the new water package and HIPPS bypass management, and a management-of-change record for the
changed relief basis. The HIPPS bypass is a SIL-3 function and must be under strict permit control for
the life of the modification.

8.6 Contracting and resourcing
The study assumes an EPCm contract for the shutdown with vendor packages for the compressor re-
rate, the separator internals, the HIPPS and the water package. Performance guarantees should sit
with the vendors for the internals K factor, the water-package outlet quality and the HIPPS SIL. The
owner retains the relief and SIL studies and the management of change.

10 Independent HAZOP
10.1 Method and independence
The completed Rev B design pack — basis of design, DEXPI PFD, equipment and material schedule,
relief and HIPPS basis, and the 42-week plan — was frozen and issued to a HAZOP team that did not
prepare it. The team worked node by node with the guidewords NO/NOT, MORE, LESS, REVERSE, AS
WELL AS, PART OF and OTHER THAN, covering normal operation, startup, shutdown, maintenance and
utility failure. The chair sequenced the process and safety roles and the scribe recorded the
worksheet; the designer did not vote on findings. The worksheet is Appendix E and the workbook
HAZOP sheet.

This is a study-level HAZOP on a screening design, not a formal IEC 61882 workshop with a full multi-
discipline owner team. It is independent of the design author, but it is not a substitute for the owner's
pre-FEED or detailed-design HAZOP. Its purpose is to test whether the FEED conclusions survive a
structured challenge, and to leave a traceable action list.

12 Conclusions and recommendations
12.1 Conclusions
1. The facility, not the reservoir, sets the rate: 137.1 MMscfd against a 163.4 MMscfd well potential,
with the HP separator at 99.8 %, the compressor at 98.1 % and the produced-water plant at 92.7 % of
their limits.

2. The capacity chain has a hidden third link: a gas-side package without the water upgrade is capped
at 147.8 MMscfd and is slightly negative in NPV.

3. The flare, not the PSV, is the relief gate: the existing PSV passes the +18 % case at 86.7 %, but the
flare overloads at 107.5 %.

4. The HIPPS + water package is the best core option: 36 MUSD, four weeks, 24.8 MUSD NPV at 10 %
and payback in year one, with the flare/header upgrade as the 42 MUSD fallback.

5. The project is a gas project with a condensate co-product: 73.7 % of the discounted revenue is gas,
and the condensate-only case loses 36.2 MUSD.

6. The fourth stage is close to neutral and should be deferred; the stabiliser fails because its two-week
shutdown extension defers 11.0 MUSD.

12.2 Recommendations
R1. Approve the HIPPS + water package for detailed design, with the flare/header upgrade as the
relief fallback and a gate on the HIPPS safety case.

R2. Commission the dynamic relief study and the SIL verification immediately; the 35 % credit is the
package's principal technical assumption.

R3. Place the compressor re-rate purchase order by week 8 to protect the week-32 shutdown; it is the
critical path.

R4. Write vendor performance guarantees for the separator internals K factor, the water-package
outlet quality and the HIPPS SIL.

R5. Verify the +18 % flare radiation contour (82.4 m to 4.73 kW/m²) against the platform sterile area
before selecting the fallback.

R6. Revisit the fourth stage at a higher condensate price or in the following shutdown; do not carry
the stabiliser in this window.

R7. Confirm the incremental gas sales route and price before sanction; the economics are conditional
on 4 USD/MMBtu.
